1. **Gal‑3 as a Dual‑Faceted Modulator**  
   - In macrophages, Gal‑3 directly repairs lysosomal membranes, restoring acidification and glycolytic flux (Paper 1).  
   - In breast cancer, Gal‑3 is released via Dectin‑1 signaling and adheres to CECs, where it co‑localizes with PD‑L1 to suppress T‑cell activity (Paper 3).  
   - In adenomyosis, Gal‑3 drives TGF‑β/Smad signaling, promoting fibroblast‑to‑myofibroblast transition (Paper 4).  
   These disparate contexts illustrate Gal‑3’s capacity to sense cellular stress or pathogen‑derived signals and translate them into either metabolic re‑programming or immune checkpoint engagement.

2. **Gal‑9 as a Treg‑CD8 Crosstalk Mediator**  
   Paper 2 demonstrates that Gal‑9 expressed on CCR7‑low Tregs engages P4HB/HAVCR2/CD44 on CD8⁺ T cells, fostering exhaustion. This axis mirrors the Gal‑3/PD‑L1 interaction in breast cancer, suggesting a broader paradigm where galectins scaffold inhibitory receptor complexes on effector T cells.

3. **Stress Granule–Like Repair Mechanisms**  
   The biomimetic stress granules (Paper 1) emulate natural SGs’ “plugging” function, a concept that could be extended to other organelles or cell types where galectins mediate membrane repair or protein aggregation.

4. **Spatial Immune Architecture as a Predictive Tool**  
   Paper 5’s inflammation score, while not directly measuring galectins, highlights the importance of spatial relationships between regulatory and effector cells. Incorporating galectin expression into such spatial analyses could refine predictive accuracy.
