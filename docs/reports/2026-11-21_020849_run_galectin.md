### 1. Galectin-3 介導 KRAS 與膜脂質互作的新機制（Galectin-3 透過其糖結合域維持細胞膜磷脂酰絲氨酸（PtdSer）以促進 KRAS 的膜定位與聚集）
- **核心機制**：Gal-3 的糖結合域透過調節 SM/ceramide 平衡，維持細胞膜內側 PtdSer 的供應，進而保證 KRAS 的膜定位與聚集。其作用不涉及 PI4P、膽固醇或 GM3，而是依賴於內吞與回收路徑的正常運作。 
- **實驗亮點**：利用 CRISPR/Cas9 及小分子抑制劑精確切斷 Gal-3 CRD；結合脂質組學與高解析度影像，證實 PtdSer 供應不足直接影響 KRAS 的膜聚集；外源性 PtdSer 能夠逆轉此效應，證明 PtdSer 是關鍵介質。 
- **臨床啟示**：KRAS 驅動的腫瘤常伴隨 Gal-3 過度表達，若能抑制 Gal-3 CRD 以降低膜 PtdSer，可能削弱 KRAS 的訊號傳導，為 KRAS 正式靶向治療提供新思路。 
- **研究限制**：研究僅在體外細胞系完成，缺乏體內模型證實；未評估 KRAS 下游訊號活性與細胞增殖的直接影響；外源性 PtdSer 的補充方式與生理條件可能不完全相符；未提供 Gal-3 CRD 與 SM/ceramide 直接結合的結構證據。

### 2. Galectin-1 為 GBM 侵襲性細胞的標記與潛在治療靶點（Galectin-1 在高侵襲性GBM細胞中的表達與功能）
- **核心機制**：Galectin‑1 透過與細胞表面糖蛋白結合，調節細胞在硬度梯度中的黏附與擴散，進而促進侵襲性遷移。
- **實驗亮點**：結合單細胞RNA‑seq與細胞條碼，精準追蹤遷移過程中的細胞族群；利用機械硬度界面模型重現腦組織與腫瘤邊界的物理環境；使用Galectin‑1結合抑制劑直接驗證其功能。
- **臨床啟示**：Galectin‑1 可作為高侵襲性GBM細胞的生物標記，並且其結合位點成為潛在藥物靶點；若能開發高特異性的Galectin‑1抑制劑，或許能抑制腫瘤擴散，改善患者預後。

### 3. 跨鏈捕捉細胞表面 Galectin‑3 互作網（Galectin-3 與細胞表面 N‑糖分支依賴性互作網路）
- **核心機制**：化學交聯固定 Galectin‑3 與低親和力的 N‑糖分支受體，形成可被捕捉的細胞表面格子網；交聯後的蛋白質可在不需外源 Galectin‑3 的情況下進行純化與質譜分析。
- **實驗亮點**：
  1. 開發 xGL‑APMS，結合交聯與親和純化，實現大規模、嚴格的表面互作捕捉。
  2. 在 Jurkat 細胞中使用六種正交驗證方法（免疫沉澱、共沉澱、流式細胞術、功能抑制、CRISPR‑敲除、質譜定量）確認 125 個互作蛋白。
  3. 低/高劑量 Galectin‑3 的互作組成差異揭示了劑量依賴的格子結構調節。
  4. 直接交聯小鼠胸腺 T 細胞內源性 Galectin‑3，擴展至原代細胞模型，發現 69 個相關蛋白。
- **臨床啟示**：
  1. 這一平台可用於疾病模型（如心臟纖維化、腫瘤免疫）中 Galectin‑3 互作網的系統性描繪，為藥物靶點篩選提供實驗基礎。
  2. N‑糖分支依賴性互作的揭示提示糖基化修飾在 Galectin‑3 介導的細胞功能中扮演關鍵角色，可能成為診斷或治療的生物標誌物。
  3. 交聯技術的可擴展性使其適用於多種細胞類型與組織，為個體化醫療提供互作網絡資訊。

### 4. Galectin-3 介入阿茲海默症微膠細胞與 Aβ 互作（Galectin-3 在微膠細胞介導的神經炎症、Aβ 聚集與溶酶體處理中的角色）

## 🧬 Cross-Study Synthesis & Molecular Mechanisms

Across these studies, Galectin‑3 emerges as a master regulator of membrane organization: its CRD preserves PtdSer and sphingomyelin/ceramide balance, while crosslinking reveals that the same CRD mediates extensive glycan‑dependent surface lattices that can modulate ligand presentation and receptor clustering.  Galectin‑1, by contrast, functions primarily at the cell‑extracellular matrix interface, binding glycoproteins to remodel adhesion and motility, as seen in GBM invasion and gastric‑cancer‑induced MMT.  Both lectins converge on glycan branching and integrin signaling pathways, yet Galectin‑3 additionally interfaces with lipid rafts and endocytic recycling, whereas Galectin‑1 signals through TGF‑β/Smad and mechanical cues.  In neuroinflammation, Galectin‑3’s binding to fibrillar Aβ alters fibril morphology and secondary nucleation, linking glycan‑mediated protein aggregation to lysosomal dysfunction and neuronal death.  The shared theme is that lectin‑glycan lattices orchestrate membrane microdomains, receptor clustering, and downstream signaling that drive oncogenic or pathological processes.

## 💡 Translational & Therapeutic Implications

The discovery that Galectin‑3 CRD inhibition depletes PtdSer and KRAS from the plasma membrane opens a novel therapeutic window for KRAS‑driven cancers: small‑molecule CRD antagonists could selectively disrupt oncogenic signaling without affecting global protein‑protein interactions.  Galectin‑1 inhibitors, already in early clinical development, may now be repurposed to curb GBM invasion and gastric‑cancer peritoneal spread by blocking its binding to surface glycoproteins and TGF‑β/Smad signaling.  The crosslinking‑enabled Galectin‑3 interactome platform offers a high‑throughput biomarker discovery pipeline; surface‑lattice signatures could be imaged with PET or optical probes for early disease detection.  In Alzheimer’s disease, modulating Galectin‑3 activity may shift Aβ aggregation dynamics and improve microglial clearance, suggesting a disease‑modifying strategy.  Finally, diagnostic assays measuring Galectin‑3 or Galectin‑1 expression in patient biopsies or liquid biopsies could stratify patients for targeted lectin‑based therapies.

## 🔭 Future Perspectives & Open Research Questions

Key future directions include: (1) in vivo validation of Galectin‑3 CRD inhibitors in KRAS‑mutant tumor xenografts and patient‑derived organoids; (2) development of selective Galectin‑1 antagonists with minimal off‑target glycan binding; (3) application of the xGL‑APMS platform to human tumor and neurodegenerative tissue to map disease‑specific lectin lattices; (4) mechanistic studies on how Galectin‑3‑mediated lipid homeostasis intersects with endocytic recycling pathways; (5) exploration of combinatorial regimens pairing Galectin inhibitors with immune checkpoint blockade or targeted kinase inhibitors; and (6) longitudinal monitoring of Galectin‑1/3 levels in plasma to assess metastatic risk in gastric cancer and GBM patients.  Open questions remain about the cell‑type specificity of lectin‑glycan lattices, the reversibility of Galectin‑3‑induced lipid remodeling, and the safety profile of chronic lectin inhibition.
