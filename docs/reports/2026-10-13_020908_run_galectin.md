### 2. Galectin‑4 對 6‑O‑硫酸化 GAGs 的特異性結合（Galectin‑4 與硫酸化糖胺聚糖（GAGs）的結合機制）
- **核心機制**：Galectin‑4 的兩個 CRD（CRD1、CRD2）在結合 6‑O‑硫酸化 GAGs 時，利用其正電位位點與硫酸基形成靜電與氫鍵互作；MD 模擬顯示 HP 分子在 CRD1/CRD2 之間形成穩定的配體結合，且 6‑O‑硫酸化位點提供額外的結合能量。
- **實驗亮點**：
  1. **GAG 微陣列**：以 98 種不同結構的 GAGs（含 6‑O‑硫酸化、2‑O‑硫酸化、雙硫酸化等）為底物，利用 Cy3‑SA 進行檢測，形成熱圖顯示 Galectin‑4 對 6‑O‑硫酸化 GAGs 的高親和力。
  2. **糖凝集素微陣列**：同時測試 49 種不同糖凝集素（SIGLEC、C‑型凝集素、Galectin 家族），證實 Galectin‑4 的特異性。
  3. **組織染色**：在 C57BL/6N 小鼠皮膚切片中，使用 PE‑標記的 Galectin‑4 進行染色，顯示其主要定位於表皮細胞表面，與 GAGs 可能形成功能性互作。
  4. **MD 模擬**：使用 CHARMM36 force field，對 Galectin‑4 CRD 與 HP 進行 200 ns 模擬，觀察到持續的配體–受體互作，並計算結合能量。
- **臨床啟示**：Galectin‑4 與 6‑O‑硫酸化 GAGs 的互作可能在上皮屏障功能、黏附、信號傳導及免疫調節中扮演關鍵角色；其在腸道、皮膚等上皮組織中的表達提示其可能參與炎症性腸病、皮膚炎症等疾病的發病機制。未來可探索 Galectin‑4 為靶點的藥物或抗體，以調節 GAG‑Galectin‑4 互作，作為治療策略。
- **研究限制**：
  1. 微陣列實驗僅評估結合強度，未提供結合親和力（Kd）數值；
  2. MD 模擬使用單一 HP 分子，未考慮多聚體或其他 GAG 變體；
  3. 組織染色僅顯示定位，缺乏功能性驗證（如細胞黏附、信號傳導實驗）。

- **實驗亮點**：綜述多項體外與體內模型，展示抗體‑Lectin融合體可特異性結合腫瘤細胞表面高表達的Tn、STn抗原；Lectin‑藥物共轭在小鼠腫瘤模型中提高藥物濃度並降低系統毒性；糖基化靶向CAR‑T在PD‑L1‑/‑Tn雙陽性腫瘤中顯示出更高的增殖與細胞毒性。

### 4. RT‑Galectin‑9：免疫抑制與治療突破（RT誘導的Galectin‑9上調及其免疫抑制機制）
- **核心機制**：RT誘導腫瘤細胞與髓系細胞表達Galectin‑9，Gal‑9與T細胞表面TIM‑3結合，觸發T細胞耗竭訊號；同時Gal‑9促進MDSC、M2巨噬細胞與Treg的招募與活化，形成全局免疫抑制環境。
- **實驗亮點**：使用雙側腫瘤模型證明局部RT可同步上調遠端腫瘤中的Gal‑9；單細胞RNA‑seq揭示Gal‑9高表達細胞主要為髓系細胞；Gal‑9阻斷抗體與PD‑1抑制劑聯合治療顯著提升CD8⁺T細胞浸潤並降低MDSC、M2巨噬細胞與Treg比例。
- **臨床啟示**：Gal‑9作為RT誘導的免疫檢查點，可作為RT後治療的靶點；Gal‑9阻斷與PD‑1聯合可擴大RT的abscopal效應，為腫瘤免疫治療提供新策略。

### 5. Gal9C‑TIM‑3 互作的優勢機制（Gal9C 與 IgV‑TIM‑3 的高親和力多價結合）
- **核心機制**：Gal9C 透過其雙重結合位點與 IgV‑TIM‑3 形成多價結合，產生高 avidity，且不易被乳糖競爭。- **實驗亮點**：19F NMR 競爭實驗顯示，即使 Gal3 以 6 倍過量存在，Gal9C 的 19F 信號仍被 IgV‑TIM‑3 強烈影響，證明其結合優勢。- **臨床啟示**：Gal9C 對 TIM‑3 的高親和力提示其在免疫調節及腫瘤免疫治療中的潛在靶點；若能抑制此互作，可能提升免疫檢查點抑制劑的療效。

## 🧬 Cross-Study Synthesis & Molecular Mechanisms

Across the five studies, galectins converge on glycan‑mediated immune regulation but diverge in tissue context and therapeutic leverage.
- **Gal-3**: Expected to act as a systemic inflammatory sentinel; its lack of elevation in DFI suggests that local tissue expression or post‑translational modifications may be more relevant, or that the foot microenvironment dampens systemic release.
- **Gal-4**: Its selective binding to 6‑O‑sulfated GAGs positions it as a gatekeeper of epithelial integrity and a potential modulator of mucosal immunity. The dual CRDs provide a scaffold for multivalent interactions, a theme echoed in Gal‑9’s TIM‑3 engagement.
- **Gal-9**: Functions as a radiation‑induced checkpoint, amplifying the immunosuppressive milieu via TIM‑3, MDSC, and Treg pathways. Its high‑avidity binding (as shown by 19F‑NMR) explains why Gal‑9 blockade outcompetes Gal‑3 for TIM‑3.
- **Shared Pathways**: All three lectins influence T‑cell exhaustion and myeloid cell polarization, but Gal‑9 uniquely couples these effects to RT‑induced DNA damage signals. The engineered lectin constructs described in the review could be tailored to target Gal‑4’s GAG interactions or Gal‑9’s TIM‑3 axis, creating a unified strategy to re‑educate the tumor microenvironment.

## 💡 Translational & Therapeutic Implications

1. **Diagnostic**: Serum Gal‑3 is unlikely to serve as a biomarker for DFI severity; future work should focus on tissue‑specific assays or imaging of Gal‑3 expression.
2. **Therapeutic**: Gal‑9 blockade, especially when combined with PD‑1 inhibitors, shows promise for enhancing radiotherapy efficacy and inducing abscopal effects. Early‑phase trials of Gal‑9 antibodies are warranted.
3. **Targeted Delivery**: Gal‑4’s affinity for 6‑O‑sulfated GAGs could be exploited to deliver drugs or nanoparticles to epithelial surfaces, potentially benefiting skin or mucosal diseases.
4. **Engineered Lectins**: Antibody‑lectin chimeras and lectin‑drug conjugates that recognize tumor‑specific glycans (e.g., Tn/STn) could be integrated with checkpoint blockade to achieve synergistic anti‑tumor immunity.
5. **Biomarker Development**: 19F‑NMR assays for Gal‑9/TIM‑3 interactions could serve as a platform for screening patient‑specific checkpoint engagement and monitoring therapeutic response.

## 🔭 Future Perspectives & Open Research Questions

Future research should address several open questions:
- **Longitudinal Dynamics**: Serial measurements of Gal‑3 in DFI patients, coupled with tissue biopsies, will clarify whether local expression predicts outcomes.
- **GAG‑Galectin Interactome**: Expanding GAG microarrays to include disease‑specific sulfation patterns may uncover novel Gal‑4 targets in fibrosis or cancer.
- **Gal‑9 in Diverse Tumors**: Comparative studies across tumor types will determine whether Gal‑9 induction is universal to RT or context‑dependent.
- **Combination Therapies**: Clinical trials combining Gal‑9 blockade with RT, PD‑1/PD‑L1 inhibitors, and engineered lectin constructs will test the translational potential highlighted in the review.
- **Structural‑Functional Mapping**: High‑resolution cryo‑EM or X‑ray structures of Gal‑9/TIM‑3 complexes, guided by 19F‑NMR data, will inform rational design of next‑generation checkpoint inhibitors.
- **Safety Profiling**: Long‑term safety of Gal‑9 blockade, especially regarding autoimmunity and off‑target effects on Gal‑3 or Gal‑4 pathways, must be rigorously evaluated.
