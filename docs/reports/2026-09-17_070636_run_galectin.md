  4. **治療干預**：隨機對照 2024 年 3‑期試驗，對 120 例重症 CRSwNP 施以 dupilumab 或 anti‑IL‑5，觀察 12 週 Gal‑10/CLC 變化。
- **臨床啟示**：
  1. **診斷工具**：Gal‑10/CLC 可作為非侵入性血清或鼻腔分泌物檢測指標，協助判斷嗜酸性炎症程度，並於術前評估復發風險。

## 🎯 Executive Summary

1. **Cardiac remodeling in elite weightlifters** – Imaging and biomarker data reveal a predominantly physiological hypertrophic response, with no elevation of circulating galectin‑3, suggesting that exercise‑induced cardiac changes are not driven by the profibrotic lectin pathway.
2. **Galectin‑10 in CRSwNP** – High tissue and serum Gal‑10 correlates with eosinophil burden and disease severity; postoperative recurrence is predicted by residual Gal‑10/CLC levels, and anti‑IL‑5/IL‑5Rα biologics markedly reduce these markers.
3. **Eosinophil subtype gene expression after allergen challenge** – Transcriptomic profiling identifies differential expression of lectin‑encoding genes, including Gal‑10, across eosinophil subsets, hinting at functional heterogeneity in allergic inflammation.
4. **RIT GTPases regulation** – Novel biochemical insights reveal that RIT1/2 interact with membrane phospholipids and may modulate downstream lectin signaling cascades, though direct links to galectins remain to be established.
5. **Modified citrus pectin (MCP) and Gal‑3 in NASH** – MCP administration reduces hepatic Gal‑3, ameliorating steatohepatitis and insulin resistance in a murine model, underscoring Gal‑3 as a therapeutic target in metabolic liver disease.

## 🧬 Cross-Study Synthesis & Molecular Mechanisms

Across the five studies, galectin‑10 emerges as a central mediator of eosinophil‑driven tissue remodeling in CRSwNP, while galectin‑3 is implicated in metabolic and fibrotic pathways in NASH. The absence of galectin‑3 elevation in exercise‑induced cardiac hypertrophy suggests that physiological remodeling bypasses the profibrotic lectin axis, contrasting with pathological states. Transcriptomic data from eosinophil subtypes reveal that Gal‑10 expression is tightly linked to the activation state of eosinophils, potentially modulated by cytokine milieu (IL‑5, IL‑13). RIT GTPases, through their membrane‑anchored signaling, may influence galectin‑mediated adhesion and migration, offering a mechanistic bridge between intracellular GTPase activity and extracellular lectin functions. Together, these findings delineate a spectrum where galectins act as both biomarkers and effectors, with their activity context‑dependent on cell type, stimulus, and disease state.

## 💡 Translational & Therapeutic Implications

1. **Diagnostic** – Serum Gal‑10 and CLC quantification could serve as a non‑invasive predictor of postoperative recurrence in CRSwNP, guiding surveillance intensity.
2. **Therapeutic** – MCP‑based Gal‑3 inhibition demonstrates efficacy in preclinical NASH, paving the way for clinical trials of dietary lectin modulators in metabolic liver disease.
3. **Cardiac** – The lack of galectin‑3 upregulation in elite athletes suggests that monitoring Gal‑3 may help differentiate physiological from pathological hypertrophy in sports cardiology.
4. **Immunomodulation** – Targeting Gal‑10 with monoclonal antibodies or small‑molecule inhibitors could complement existing biologics (anti‑IL‑5/IL‑5Rα) to reduce polyp recurrence.
5. **Drug development** – RIT GTPase inhibitors might indirectly modulate galectin‑mediated pathways, offering a novel class of therapeutics for fibrotic and inflammatory disorders.

## 🔭 Future Perspectives & Open Research Questions

Future research should focus on longitudinal studies to validate Gal‑10 as a prognostic marker in CRSwNP and to assess the durability of MCP‑mediated Gal‑3 suppression in human NASH. Standardization of ELISA and imaging protocols for galectin quantification will be essential for cross‑study comparability. The mechanistic link between RIT GTPases and galectin signaling warrants deeper exploration, potentially via proteomic interaction mapping and functional assays in relevant cell types. Additionally, the role of galectins in exercise‑induced cardiac remodeling remains under‑explored; integrating omics data from athletes could uncover protective lectin signatures. Finally, combinatorial strategies that target both cytokine pathways and lectin mediators may yield synergistic benefits in eosinophilic and fibrotic diseases.
