🧬 Cross-Study Synthesis & Molecular Mechanisms

The five studies converge on a central theme: galectins orchestrate cell‑cell communication in both neuro‑degenerative and cardiovascular contexts. In POAG, the TIM‑3/Galectin‑9 axis promotes retinal ganglion cell apoptosis and microglial activation, mirroring the Tau‑microglia interaction where Tau pathology may trigger complement‑mediated pruning. In the gut, Tsgal exploits MUC13 to hijack ROCK2/MAPK, a pathway also implicated in cardiac fibroblast activation post‑MI. Galectin‑3, a key player in post‑infarct inflammation, is also a biomarker of exercise‑induced cardiac stress, underscoring its dual role as a mediator and marker across tissues. Shared downstream effectors—NF‑κB, MAPK, and complement components—suggest that galectin‑mediated lectin binding initiates a conserved inflammatory cascade that can be modulated therapeutically.

💡 Translational & Therapeutic Implications

Clinical translation is poised to benefit from these insights. In glaucoma, routine ELISA for serum sTIM‑3 and Galectin‑9 could augment VF/OCT monitoring, enabling earlier intervention. Targeting the Galectin‑9/TIM‑3 interaction with monoclonal antibodies or small‑molecule inhibitors may halt microglial‑driven neurodegeneration. For Trichinella infection, vaccine candidates that block Tsgal or MUC13, or pharmacologic ROCK2/MAPK inhibitors, could prevent larval invasion. In cardiology, galectin‑3 assays may refine risk stratification post‑MI and guide anti‑fibrotic therapy; in athletes, distinguishing physiological from pathological galectin‑3 elevations will improve diagnostic accuracy. These applications hinge on robust, longitudinal validation and the development of specific galectin modulators.

🔭 Future Perspectives & Open Research Questions

Future research must address several gaps: (1) Prospective, multicenter studies to confirm serum sTIM‑3/Galectin‑9 as predictive biomarkers in diverse glaucoma populations. (2) In vivo models to establish causality between Tau pathology and microglial over‑phagocytosis, and to test complement inhibitors. (3) Animal studies of Tsgal to validate the MUC13‑ROCK2/MAPK axis and assess vaccine efficacy. (4) Integration of single‑cell RNA‑seq and proteomics to map galectin‑3‑mediated networks in post‑MI hearts and delineate therapeutic targets. (5) Standardized reference ranges for galectin‑3 in athletes, accounting for training load and sex differences. Addressing these questions will unlock galectin‑centric diagnostics and therapeutics across ophthalmology, neurology, infectious disease, and cardiology.
