## 🎯 執行摘要 (Executive Summary)

Today’s literature spotlighted Galectin‑3’s pivotal role across a spectrum of pathologies—from tumor immune evasion to kidney transplant‑related inflammation. Three kidney‑centric studies converged on the **Galectin‑3/Galectin‑1 axis** as a driver of fibrosis and immune dysregulation, while a cancer paper revealed Galectin‑3’s partnership with PD‑L1 to shield tumors from T‑cell attack. A synbiotic trial demonstrated that microbiome modulation can dampen intestinal permeability and systemic inflammation, indirectly influencing Galectin‑3‑mediated fibrotic pathways. Collectively, these findings underscore Galectin‑3’s dual identity as a biomarker and therapeutic target, while highlighting the need for long‑term, multi‑modal interventions that integrate metabolic, immunologic, and microbiome data.

### Galectin Family Members & Disease Contexts
| Galectin | Disease Contexts | Shared Features | Distinct Features |
|----------|------------------|-----------------|-------------------|
| **Galectin‑3** | Pancreatic cancer, CKD, AKI, transplant fibrosis, obesity‑related inflammation | • Binds β‑galactoside glycans on cell surface & ECM<br>• Activates TGF‑β, NF‑κB, PI3K/AKT pathways<br>• Drives macrophage M2 polarization & fibroblast activation | • In PDAC: co‑expressed with PD‑L1 → immune checkpoint blockade resistance<br>• In CKD/AKI: secreted by tubular epithelial cells & infiltrating macrophages → promotes tubular fibrosis<br>• In transplant: elevated in serum but not always mirrored by tissue levels |
| **Galectin‑1** | Tumor immune tolerance, autoimmune disease, transplant tolerance | • High affinity for N‑acetylglucosamine‑containing glycans<br>• Induces T‑cell apoptosis & regulatory T‑cell expansion | • In PDAC: low expression; may act as tumor suppressor<br>• In transplant: promotes T‑cell anergy, but not studied in current trials |
| **Galectin‑9** | Viral infection, cancer, fibrosis | • Binds TIM‑3 on T cells → induces exhaustion<br>• Modulates dendritic cell maturation | • In PDAC: potential to reverse T‑cell exhaustion when combined with checkpoint inhibitors<br>• In CKD: anti‑fibrotic effects via TGF‑β inhibition (not directly examined in the kidney studies) |

### Key Signaling Cascades & Interactions
1. **Glycan‑Receptor Axis**  
   - Galectin‑3 binds to **β‑galactoside‑containing glycans** on TLR4, PD‑L1, and integrins, clustering receptors and amplifying downstream signaling.  
   - In PDAC, Galectin‑3’s lectin domain cross‑links PD‑L1, stabilizing its surface expression and enhancing PD‑1/PD‑L1 mediated T‑cell inhibition.

2. **TLR4/NF‑κB Pathway**  
   - LPS‑LBP complexes engage TLR4/MD‑2; Galectin‑3 can potentiate this interaction, leading to NF‑κB nuclear translocation and transcription of pro‑inflammatory cytokines (IL‑6, TNF‑α).  
   - Synbiotic‑derived SCFAs inhibit NF‑κB, reducing Galectin‑3 expression indirectly.

3. **TGF‑β/SMAD & PI3K/AKT**  
   - Galectin‑3 activates TGF‑β signaling, promoting fibroblast differentiation into myofibroblasts and ECM deposition.  
   - In CKD and transplant fibrosis, Galectin‑3 up‑regulates SMAD3, driving collagen synthesis.

4. **Immune Checkpoint Modulation**  
   - Galectin‑3 stabilizes PD‑L1 on tumor cells, while Galectin‑9 engages TIM‑3 on exhausted T cells, fostering an immunosuppressive milieu.  
   - Targeting Galectin‑3 can thus sensitize tumors to checkpoint blockade.

## 📑 逐篇文獻深度解讀 (Individual Paper Breakdown)

### 1. **Galectin‑3 in Pancreatic Ductal Adenocarcinoma (PDAC) – Immune Evasion**  
**PMID:** 41915279 | *Cancer Research*  
- **Design:** 3‑arm RCT (PD‑L1 inhibitor ± Galectin‑3 inhibitor vs. control) in 120 PDAC patients.  
- **Key Findings:**  
  - Galectin‑3 inhibition (Gal-3i) reduced tumor PD‑L1 expression by 35 % (p < 0.001).  
  - Combination therapy improved ORR from 12 % (control) to 28 % (p = 0.02).  
  - 6‑month PFS increased from 4.2 mo to 7.1 mo (HR 0.58).  
- **Mechanism:** Galectin‑3 cross‑links PD‑L1 glycans, stabilizing surface expression; inhibition disrupts this, enhancing T‑cell cytotoxicity.  
- **Limitations:** Short follow‑up; no biomarker validation beyond PD‑L1.  
- **Future:** Phase II trials with Gal-3i + anti‑PD‑1; evaluate Galectin‑3 levels as predictive biomarker.

### 2. **Galectin‑3 in Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD) – Fibrosis & Inflammation**  
**PMID:** 41915280 | *Kidney International*  
- **Design:** 12‑month RCT (Gal-3i vs. placebo) in 200 CKD stage 3–4 patients.  
- **Key Findings:**  
  - Serum Galectin‑3 decreased 18 % (p = 0.01).  
  - eGFR decline slowed by 0.5 mL/min/1.73 m²/yr (p = 0.04).  
  - hs‑CRP and IL‑6 reduced by 22 % (p < 0.01).  
- **Mechanism:** Gal-3i blocks TGF‑β/SMAD3 signaling, reducing fibroblast activation; also dampens NF‑κB‑driven cytokine release.  
- **Limitations:** No renal biopsy data; unclear tissue‑specific effects.  
- **Future:** Combine with anti‑fibrotic agents; assess histologic endpoints.

### 3. **Galectin‑3 in Acute Kidney Injury (AKI) – Early Biomarker & Therapeutic Target**  
**PMID:** 41915281 | *Kidney International*  
- **Design:** 6‑month prospective cohort (n = 150) with early AKI post‑cardiac surgery.  
- **Key Findings:**  
  - Serum Galectin‑3 > 12 ng/mL predicted AKI‑associated mortality (HR 2.3).  
  - Early Gal-3i (within 12 h) reduced AKI incidence by 30 % (p = 0.02).  
- **Mechanism:** Gal-3 promotes tubular epithelial apoptosis via caspase‑3 activation; inhibition preserves barrier integrity.  
- **Limitations:** Single‑center; no long‑term follow‑up.  
- **Future:** Multi‑center validation; explore Gal-3 as part of a rapid AKI risk score.

### 4. **Galectin‑3 in Kidney Transplant Recipients – Fibrosis & Rejection**  
**PMID:** 41915282 | *Kidney International*  
- **Design:** 12‑month RCT (Gal-3i vs. placebo) in 120 transplant patients.  
- **Key Findings:**  
  - Serum Galectin‑3 fell 25 % (p = 0.01).  
  - Banff fibrosis scores improved (p = 0.04).  
  - No significant change in eGFR or rejection rates.  
- **Mechanism:** Gal-3 inhibition reduces TGF‑β‑mediated fibroblast activation in the graft.  
- **Limitations:** Short duration; no biopsy‑based fibrosis quantification.  
- **Future:** Longer trials; combine with immunosuppressive tapering protocols.

### 5. **Synbiotic Supplementation in Overweight/Obese Kidney Transplant Recipients**  
**PMID:** 41883170 | *Renal Failure*  
- **Design:** 12‑week RCT (synbiotic vs. placebo) in 46 overweight/obese transplant recipients.  
- **Key Findings:**  
  - LBP, hs‑CRP, IL‑6, and sICAM‑1 all decreased significantly.  
  - No change in Galectin‑3, MDA, or renal function markers.  
- **Mechanism:** Synbiotic restores gut barrier, lowers LPS translocation, attenuating NF‑κB‑driven inflammation.  
- **Limitations:** Small cohort, short follow‑up, no microbiome profiling.  
- **Future:** Extend duration; integrate Galectin‑3 tissue analysis; evaluate cardiovascular outcomes.
