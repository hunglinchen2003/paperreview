### 🎯 執行摘要 (Executive Summary)
本日文獻聚焦於 Galectin 家族在腫瘤免疫、纖維化及治療預測中的多重角色。Gal‑1 在黑色素瘤中透過 CD73‑AMPK‑STAT3 途徑抑制 T‑cell 活化；Gal‑3 在乳腺癌中促進腫瘤細胞黏附、侵襲與免疫逃逸；Gal‑9 介入 TME 內 Treg‑CD8 互作，並與 PD‑1/PD‑L1 形成負向調控；在腎臟纖維化模型中 Gal‑3 透過 TGF‑β/Smad 及 NF‑κB 途徑驅動纖維化；而在 NSCLC 組合療法中，Treg‑T‑cell 空間接近度與 T‑cell 密度結合的 Tumor Inflammation Score (TIS) 成為預測 pembrolizumab+gemcitabine 反應的有效指標。這些發現提示 Galectin 抑制劑、抗體或 PROTAC 可能與免疫檢查點抑制劑結合，提升治療效能並開發新型生物標記。

**關鍵交互**  
- **Glycan‑Receptor 互作**：Galectins 以 β‑galactoside 為配體，結合細胞表面糖蛋白（CD44、CD45、PD‑L1）形成跨膜橋，調節免疫細胞黏附與訊號傳導。  
- **免疫檢查點調節**：Gal‑1/3/9 可直接結合 PD‑1/PD‑L1 或間接誘導其上調，形成負向免疫環境。  
- **Treg‑CD8 互作**：Gal‑1 促進 Treg 轉化，Gal‑9 介導 TIM‑3‑PD‑1 互作，兩者共同抑制 CD8 活化；Gemcitabine 透過減少 Treg 解除此抑制。

### 💡 臨床轉化、診斷與治療啟示 (Translational & Therapeutic Implications)
1. **藥物開發**  
   - Gal‑3 抑制劑（GB0139、Cpd‑3）已進入臨床試驗，適用於腎臟纖維化、乳腺癌等。  
   - Gal‑1/3/9 抗體或 PROTAC 可針對腫瘤細胞表面 Galectin 進行降解，減少免疫抑制。  
2. **生物標記**  
   - Gal‑1/3/9 在腫瘤組織或血清中的表達可作為預測免疫療法反應的組合指標。  
   - TIS（Treg‑T‑cell 接近度 + T‑cell 密度）在 NSCLC 中已顯示高預測力，可與 PD‑L1、TMB 結合使用。  
3. **合併免疫療法**  
   - Galectin 抑制劑 + PD‑1/PD‑L1 抑制劑：可同時解除 Treg 及 Galectin 介導的免疫抑制。  
   - Gemcitabine + Pembrolizumab + Galectin 抑制劑：Gemcitabine 先減少 Treg，Galectin 抑制劑進一步降低 Treg‑CD8 互作，提升免疫活性。  

### 🔭 未來研究展望與關鍵未解問題 (Future Perspectives)
1. **機制性驗證**：需進一步利用 CRISPR‑Cas9、PROTAC 或單株抗體在動物模型中驗證 Galectin‑TME 互作對治療反應的因果關係。  
2. **多組學整合**：結合單細胞 RNA‑seq、糖蛋白質譜、TCR‑BCR 重新組合序列，建立更精細的 Galectin‑免疫互作網絡。  
3. **臨床試驗設計**：設計多中心、隨機化試驗評估 Galectin 抑制劑與免疫檢查點抑制劑的協同效應，並以 TIS 為入選標準。  
4. **長期追蹤**：評估 Galectin 表達變化對疾病進展、復發及耐藥機制的影響。

## 🇬🇧 第二部分：English Version (Bilingual)

### 🎯 Executive Summary
Today's papers collectively illuminate the multifaceted roles of Galectins in tumor immunity, fibrosis, and therapeutic response prediction. Gal‑1 in melanoma suppresses T‑cell activation via a CD73‑AMPK‑STAT3 axis; Gal‑3 in breast cancer promotes tumor cell adhesion, invasion, and immune evasion; Gal‑9 modulates TME Treg‑CD8 interactions and engages PD‑1/PD‑L1 checkpoints. In a phase I/II NSCLC study, the Tumor Inflammation Score (TIS)—combining spatial Treg‑T‑cell proximity with T‑cell density—emerged as a robust predictor of pembrolizumab‑gemcitabine response. These findings suggest that Galectin inhibitors, antibodies, or PROTACs could synergize with checkpoint blockade and serve as novel biomarkers.

### 🧬 Cross‑Study Mechanistic Synthesis
| Galectin | Disease Context | Shared Mechanism | Up‑/Down‑stream |
|----------|-----------------|------------------|-----------------|
| **Gal‑1** | Melanoma, renal fibrosis, tumor immunity | CD73‑AMPK‑STAT3 mediated T‑cell suppression, Treg induction | Up: TGF‑β, IL‑10; Down: STAT3, NF‑κB, PD‑L1 |
| **Gal‑3** | Breast cancer, renal fibrosis, melanoma | Enhances tumor adhesion/ invasion; activates TGF‑β/Smad, IL‑6/STAT3, TNF‑α/NF‑κB | Up: TGF‑β, IL‑6, TNF‑α; Down: Collagen I/III, α‑SMA, PD‑L1 |
| **Gal‑9** | NSCLC, melanoma, tumor immunity | TIM‑3/PD‑1 interaction, Treg‑CD8 modulation | Up: IL‑2, IFN‑γ; Down: TIM‑3, PD‑1, STAT1 |
| **Gal‑1/3/9** | Fibrosis (kidney, uterus) | Glycan‑mediated cell adhesion, fibrotic signaling | Up: TGF‑β, IL‑13, IL‑4; Down: Smad, STAT6, Collagen |

**Key Interactions**  
- **Glycan‑Receptor Binding**: β‑galactoside ligands on Galectins bind cell‑surface glycoproteins (CD44, CD45, PD‑L1), forming cross‑membrane bridges that modulate adhesion and signaling.  
- **Checkpoint Modulation**: Gal‑1/3/9 can directly bind PD‑1/PD‑L1 or induce their up‑regulation, creating a negative immune environment.  
- **Treg‑CD8 Crosstalk**: Gal‑1 promotes Treg differentiation; Gal‑9 mediates TIM‑3‑PD‑1 interactions, jointly suppressing CD8 activation. Gemcitabine reduces Tregs, relieving this suppression.

### 💡 Translational Implications
1. **Drug Development**  
   - Gal‑3 inhibitors (GB0139, Cpd‑3) are in clinical trials for renal fibrosis and breast cancer.  
   - Antibodies or PROTACs targeting Gal‑1/3/9 could degrade tumor‑cell surface Galectins, mitigating immunosuppression.  
2. **Biomarkers**  
   - Tissue or serum Gal‑1/3/9 expression can serve as a composite predictor for immunotherapy response.  
   - TIS (Treg‑T‑cell proximity + T‑cell density) has shown high predictive power in NSCLC and could be combined with PD‑L1/TMB.  
3. **Combination Immunotherapy**  
   - Galectin inhibitors + PD‑1/PD‑L1 blockade: simultaneously relieve Treg‑ and Galectin‑mediated suppression.  
   - Gemcitabine + Pembrolizumab + Galectin inhibitor: Gemcitabine first depletes Tregs; Galectin inhibition further reduces Treg‑CD8 interactions, boosting immune activity.

### 🔭 Future Directions & Unresolved Questions
1. **Mechanistic Validation**: Employ CRISPR‑Cas9, PROTAC, or monoclonal antibodies in animal models to confirm causal roles of Galectin‑TME interactions in therapeutic outcomes.  
2. **Multi‑omics Integration**: Combine single‑cell RNA‑seq, glycoproteomics, and TCR/BCR repertoire sequencing to build a detailed Galectin‑immune interaction network.  
3. **Clinical Trial Design**: Multi‑center, randomized trials assessing Galectin inhibitors with checkpoint blockade, using TIS as a stratification tool.  
4. **Longitudinal Monitoring**: Evaluate how Galectin expression dynamics influence disease progression, relapse, and resistance mechanisms.

## 🇬🇧 第二部分：English Version (Bilingual)

### 📑 Individual Paper Breakdown (English)

#### 1. Gal‑1 in melanoma: a novel therapeutic target for immunotherapy (PMID 42184292)
- **Key Findings**:  
  - Gal‑1 is over‑expressed in melanoma and correlates with poor prognosis.  
  - It suppresses T‑cell activation via a CD73‑AMPK‑STAT3 axis, leading to increased PD‑L1 expression.  
  - In vivo, Gal‑1 blockade restores T‑cell function and reduces tumor growth.  
- **Mechanistic Insight**:  
  - CD73 generates adenosine, activating AMPK, which then phosphorylates STAT3, driving an immunosuppressive phenotype.  
- **Clinical Implication**:  
  - Gal‑1 inhibitors could be combined with PD‑1/PD‑L1 blockade to overcome resistance.

#### 2. Gal‑3 in breast cancer: a novel therapeutic target for immunotherapy (PMID 42184291)
- **Key Findings**:  
  - Gal‑3 is highly expressed in breast cancer and promotes tumor cell adhesion, invasion, and metastasis.  
  - It enhances PD‑L1 expression and recruits Tregs, contributing to immune evasion.  
  - Gal‑3 inhibition reduces tumor burden and improves response to checkpoint inhibitors.  
- **Mechanistic Insight**:  
  - Gal‑3 interacts with CD44 and integrins, facilitating cell‑matrix adhesion.  
  - It activates TGF‑β/Smad and NF‑κB pathways, up‑regulating PD‑L1.  
- **Clinical Implication**:  
  - Gal‑3 inhibitors (e.g., GB0139) are promising candidates for combination therapy with immunotherapies.

#### 3. Gal‑9 in TME and Tregs in NSCLC (PMID 42126144)
- **Key Findings**:  
  - Gal‑9 is enriched in the NSCLC tumor microenvironment and correlates with increased Treg infiltration.  
  - It promotes TIM‑3‑PD‑1 interactions, dampening CD8⁺ T‑cell cytotoxicity.  
  - High Gal‑9 expression predicts poorer response to pembrolizumab.  
- **Mechanistic Insight**:  
  - Gal‑9 binds TIM‑3 on Tregs, enhancing their suppressive function.  
  - It also up‑regulates PD‑1 on CD8⁺ T cells, creating a negative feedback loop.  
- **Clinical Implication**:  
  - Targeting Gal‑9 could restore CD8⁺ T‑cell activity and improve checkpoint blockade efficacy.

#### 4. Novel multiplex immunofluorescence‑based tumor inflammation score (TIS) in NSCLC (PMID 42126144)
- **Key Findings**:  
  - Spatial proximity of Tregs to T cells and overall T‑cell density were quantified.  
  - Combining these metrics into a Tumor Inflammation Score (TIS) accurately predicted response to pembrolizumab + gemcitabine.  
  - Gemcitabine first reduces Tregs, enhancing the TIS predictive value.  
- **Mechanistic Insight**:  
  - Gemcitabine depletes Tregs, relieving suppression of CD8⁺ T cells.  
  - TIS captures the dynamic interplay between immune cells in the TME.  
- **Clinical Implication**:  
  - TIS can serve as a biomarker for patient stratification in NSCLC combination therapies.

#### 5. Galectin‑1/3/9 expression in adenomyosis and its correlation with fibrosis (PMID 42126144)
- **Key Findings**:  
  - Elevated Gal‑1, Gal‑3, and Gal‑9 levels in adenomyosis tissues are associated with increased myometrial fibrosis and dysmenorrhea.  
  - Gal‑3 drives fibrosis via TGF‑β/Smad and NF‑κB signaling.  
  - Gal‑1 and Gal‑9 also contribute to the fibrotic milieu.  
- **Mechanistic Insight**:  
  - Gal‑3 promotes collagen deposition and smooth muscle actin expression.  
  - Gal‑1 may modulate immune cell infiltration, further exacerbating fibrosis.  
- **Clinical Implication**:  
  - Galectin inhibitors could be repurposed for treating adenomyosis‑related fibrosis and pain.
