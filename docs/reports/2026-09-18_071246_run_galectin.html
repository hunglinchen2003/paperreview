🧬 Cross-Study Synthesis & Molecular Mechanisms

  • Shared pathways: All studies converge on Galectin‑3 (LGALS3) or Galectin‑9 as pivotal modulators of immune evasion and metabolic reprogramming. The CD44–Galectin‑9 axis amplifies TGF‑β/Smad3 signaling, a pathway also exploited by tumor‑derived exosomes to promote immune suppression.
  • Mechanistic overlap: YBX1‑mediated m5C stabilization of LGALS3 mirrors the CD44–Galectin‑9 complex’s allosteric enhancement of receptor activity—both represent post‑transcriptional or protein‑protein interactions that potentiate galectin signaling.
  • Cross‑organ relevance: While the ocular study focuses on neuroinflammation, the same Galectin‑3–phagocytosis axis is implicated in tumor‑associated macrophage function, suggesting a conserved role in phagocytic surveillance.
  • Therapeutic convergence: The inhaled LTI‑03 trial and the review both underscore galectin‑3 inhibition as a promising strategy across fibrotic and oncologic contexts, reinforcing the translational potential of targeting galectin‑mediated pathways.

💡 Translational & Therapeutic Implications

  • Immunotherapy combination: Targeting the CD44–Galectin‑9–TGF‑β–Smad3 loop could sensitize tumors to checkpoint inhibitors by dismantling the self‑regulating immune‑evasion circuit.
  • Biomarker development: Exosomal LGALS3 enriched by YBX1/m5C could serve as a liquid‑biopsy marker for aggressive colorectal cancer and guide patient selection for galectin‑3 inhibitors.
  • Drug repurposing: LTI‑03’s inhaled delivery offers a non‑systemic route to inhibit Galectin‑3 in pulmonary fibrosis; similar formulations might be adapted for ocular or systemic diseases where Galectin‑3 drives pathology.
  • Phagocytosis modulation: Modulating Galectin‑3 in Müller glia could alter neuroinflammatory responses in glaucoma, opening avenues for neuroprotective therapies.
  • Combination strategies: Co‑targeting YBX1, m5C methylation, and galectin‑3 may synergistically suppress tumor lipid metabolism and stemness, providing a multi‑layered therapeutic approach.

🔭 Future Perspectives & Open Research Questions

  • In vivo validation: The CD44–Galectin‑9 study requires animal models and patient tumor samples to confirm the autocrine loop’s relevance across cancer types.
  • Exosome heterogeneity: Future work must refine exosome isolation and labeling to definitively attribute LGALS3 cargo to M2 TAMs versus other stromal sources.
  • Ocular disease mechanisms: The functional role of Galectin‑3 in Müller glia phagocytosis remains speculative; longitudinal studies in glaucoma patients could clarify causality.
  • Therapeutic safety: Long‑term safety of galectin‑3 inhibition, especially in combination with checkpoint blockade, needs systematic assessment.
  • Biomarker standardization: Establishing robust, clinically actionable thresholds for exosomal LGALS3 and YBX1 activity will be essential for translational adoption.
