• 核心機制：Galectin-1由腫瘤細胞分泌，透過其凝集素結合細胞表面醣類，形成免疫寬恕微環境；EBV可透過AP‑1途徑上調LGALS1表達。 • 實驗亮點：採用IHC於60例HL石蠟切片，並以>50%陽性細胞為標準，結合EBV LMP‑1表達量進行統計相關性分析。 • 臨床啟示：Galectin‑1與EBV高表達之共現提示兩者可能在HL發病機制中互相促進，提示Galectin‑1作為潛在診斷或治療靶點的可能性；然而本研究未顯示其對預後的獨立影響。

2. suPAR 為 HFpEF 的上游放大器（suPAR 促進心衰與 CCR2⁺ 巨噬細胞擴增（galectin-3 標記））

核心機制：suPAR 作為上游放大器，透過與巨噬細胞表面受體結合（尚未明確識別）先行激活，提升 CCR2⁺ 免疫細胞在心臟與系統性免疫器官的擴增，進而放大 IL‑1β、TNF‑α 等炎症訊號，最終導致舒張功能障礙與肺水腫。 實驗亮點：① suPAR‑轉基因模型結合高脂+L‑NAME 兩階段心代謝刺激，模擬人類 HFpEF 的多因素病因；② 透過批量 RNA‑seq 與細胞去卷積，精準描繪心室組織的免疫與代謝轉錄變化；③ 光譜流式細胞術與 galectin‑3 免疫荧光結合，提供多層次確認 CCR2⁺ 巨噬細胞擴增的證據；④ 在體外以重組 suPAR 先行刺激巨噬細胞，證實 suPAR 不是單獨的炎症誘因，而是放大 LPS/IFN‑γ 產生的炎症反應。 臨床啟示：suPAR 可作為 HFpEF 的獨立預後指標，且 suPAR‑抗體或 CCR2‑阻斷劑可能成為新型治療策略。 研究限制：① 研究僅在小鼠模型中完成，缺乏人類心臟組織驗證；② suPAR 受體機制尚未確定；③ galectin‑3 只作為巨噬細胞標記，未直接探討其功能；④ 轉基因 suPAR 表達水平可能高於臨床實際濃度，影響結果外推性。

3. Galectin-3驅動胃部SPEM的機制（Galectin-3在胃部選擇性Cathartocytosis中的角色）

  • 受傷後Lgals3在RNA與蛋白水平上上調；
  • Galectin‑3與硫化黏液共定位於胃主細胞顆粒；
  • Galectin‑3促進其所在囊泡的Cathartocytosis；
  • 抑制細胞下調延遲Sox9表達與細胞增殖。

4. 胃上皮轉化與 Galectin-8（Galectin-8 與 CD44v 之互作及其對 STAT3 信號的調節）

  • 核心機制：Galectin-8 透過結合 CD44v 的 N‑acetyl乳糖胺基團，促進其在胃腺基底細胞膜上的定位，進而抑制 CD44v‑STAT3 軌道的活化；缺失 Galectin-8 使 CD44v 失去膜鎖定，導致 STAT3 在核內磷酸化升高。
  • 實驗亮點：利用同步化學誘導的 SPEM 模型，精確比較基因敲除與野生型小鼠；結合免疫組織化學與細胞定位技術，提供了細胞層面的直接證據。
  • 臨床啟示：低 Galectin-8 水平與胃癌預後不良相關，提示 Galectin-8 可能作為預後指標或治療靶點；調節 CD44v‑STAT3 軌道的策略或許能減緩胃上皮轉化進程。
  • 研究限制：僅在小鼠模型中觀察，缺乏人類組織驗證；未進行共沉澱或功能阻斷實驗以證實直接互作；研究僅報告 pSTAT3 的增高，未測定下游基因表達或功能影響。

5. Galectin-3 為新興 Aβ 介入靶點（Galectin-3 在阿茲海默症中調節 Aβ 聚集與神經炎症的機制）

  • 核心機制：Galectin‑3 透過其凝集素結構結合 Aβ 纖維，抑制其聚集形成斑塊；同時調節微膠質細胞活化，降低炎症因子釋放，減輕神經炎症。
  • 實驗亮點：在小鼠模型中，Galectin‑3 抑制劑顯著降低了 Aβ 斑塊密度，並伴隨微膠質細胞 M1/M2 狀態轉換，提示其雙重作用。
  • 臨床啟示：若能進一步證實其安全性與有效性，Galectin‑3 介入可作為 Aβ 介入藥物的補充或替代方案，特別是在 ARIA 風險較高的患者中。
  • 研究限制：目前僅有前臨床證據，缺乏臨床試驗；Galectin‑3 作用於多種細胞類型，可能產生非特異性效應；藥物設計與給藥方式仍待優化。

🧬 Cross-Study Synthesis & Molecular Mechanisms

Mechanism and evidence threads across papers on «galectin»:

1. Galectin-1 in Hodgkin Lymphoma (Galectin-1 expression in Classical Hodgkin Lymphoma and its association with EBV LMP-1)

• Core Insight: Galectin‑1 secreted by tumor cells binds cell‑surface glycans via its lectin domain, creating an immune‑privileged microenvironment; EBV may up‑regulate LGALS1 through an AP‑1 driven pathway. • Methodology: IHC on 60 formalin‑fixed, paraffin‑embedded HL cases, defining >50% positive cells as positive, and correlating with EBV LMP‑1 expression and clinicopathological data. • Clinical Angle: Co‑expression of Galectin‑1 and high EBV suggests a pathogenetic link in HL and points to Galectin‑1 as a potential diagnostic or therapeutic target, though its independent prognostic value remains unproven.

2. suPAR as upstream amplifier in HFpEF (suPAR-driven HFpEF exacerbation via CCR2⁺ monocyte-macrophage axis (galectin-3 as marker))

Core Insight: suPAR acts as an upstream amplifier by priming macrophages—likely via an as‑yet‑unidentified receptor—leading to expansion of CCR2⁺ monocytes/macrophages and heightened IL‑1β, TNF‑α, and NLRP3 signaling that culminate in diastolic dysfunction and pulmonary congestion. Methodology Highlights: 1) suPAR‑transgenic mice subjected to a cardiometabolic two‑hit model (high‑fat diet + L‑NAME) to recapitulate multifactorial HFpEF; 2) bulk RNA‑seq with in‑silico deconvolution to map transcriptional shifts in LV tissue; 3) spectral flow cytometry coupled with galectin‑3 immunofluorescence to validate CCR2⁺ macrophage expansion; 4) in‑vitro priming of bone‑marrow‑derived macrophages with recombinant suPAR followed by LPS/IFN‑γ to demonstrate suPAR‑mediated amplification of inflammatory cytokine production. Clinical Angle: suPAR emerges as a prognostic biomarker and a potential therapeutic target; anti‑suPAR antibodies or CCR2 antagonists could be explored for HFpEF treatment. Limitations: 1) Findings derived from a murine model; human validation is pending; 2) suPAR receptor and downstream signaling remain undefined; 3) galectin‑3 used only as a macrophage marker, not functionally interrogated; 4) transgenic suPAR expression may exceed physiological levels, limiting translational extrapolation.

3. Galectin-3 drives gastric SPEM (Galectin-3 in selective cathartocytosis)

  • Injury induces upregulation of Lgals3 at RNA and protein levels;
  • Galectin‑3 colocalizes with sulfated mucins in zymogenic granules of gastric chief cells;
  • Galectin‑3 facilitates cathartocytosis of its vesicles;
  • Inhibition of cellular downscaling delays Sox9 expression and proliferation.

4. Gastric Metaplasia (Galectin-8 interaction with CD44v and modulation of STAT3 signaling)

  • Core Insight: Galectin-8 binds the N‑acetyl lactosamine motifs on CD44v, anchoring it to the plasma membrane of SPEM cells and restraining CD44v‑STAT3 signaling; loss of Galectin-8 releases CD44v from membrane confinement, leading to increased nuclear pSTAT3.
  • Methodology Highlights: A synchronous chemically induced SPEM mouse model was used to compare Lgals8 knockout with wild‑type controls, employing immunohistochemistry and subcellular localization to provide cellular‑level evidence.
  • Clinical Angle: Low Galectin‑8 expression correlates with poor gastric cancer prognosis, suggesting Galectin‑8 as a potential prognostic marker or therapeutic target; modulating the CD44v‑STAT3 axis could mitigate gastric metaplasia progression.
  • Limitations: Findings are limited to a mouse model without human tissue validation; direct interaction was not confirmed by co‑immunoprecipitation or functional blockade; only pSTAT3 elevation was reported without downstream gene expression or functional assays.

5. Galectin-3 as a novel Aβ target (Galectin-3-mediated modulation of Aβ aggregation and neuroinflammation in Alzheimer's disease)

  • Core Insight: Galectin‑3 binds amyloid‑β fibrils via its carbohydrate recognition domain, inhibiting aggregation and promoting plaque clearance; it also modulates microglial polarization, reducing pro‑inflammatory cytokine release.
  • Methodology Highlight: In transgenic mouse models, Galectin‑3 inhibitors reduced plaque burden and shifted microglia from M1 to M2 phenotype, demonstrating dual anti‑amyloid and anti‑inflammatory effects.
  • Clinical Angle: If safety and efficacy are confirmed, Galectin‑3 targeting could complement existing Aβ therapies, especially for patients at high risk of ARIA.
  • Limitations: Evidence is limited to preclinical studies; systemic effects of Galectin‑3 inhibition are uncertain; drug delivery and pharmacokinetics require further optimization.
