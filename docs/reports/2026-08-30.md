## 中文綜合總覽

糖凝集素是一類多功能的碳水化合物結合蛋白，能在免疫、代謝、心臟及腫瘤等多種生理與病理環境中調節細胞間和細胞外基質互動。近期研究顯示，它們在免疫調控、脂肪組織重塑、心衰壓力反應以及癌細胞遷移方面扮演重要角色。

在脂肪組織中，糖凝集素‑9（Galectin‑9）特異性富集於皮下前驅細胞（APC），促進抗炎表型；CD44 的表達量則定量調節 APC 向脂肪分化的程度。外泌體標誌 CD63 進一步增強外泌體釋放並抵禦鐵誘導壓力，說明部位特異性重塑機制可能與糖凝集素訊號交互作用。在心衰中，Galectin‑3（Gal‑3）補充了傳統的 NT‑proBNP 生物標誌物，反映生物機械應力和纖維化重塑；結合 sST2 可識別高危表型，但尚缺乏隨機對照試驗證實其臨床效益。

在腫瘤細胞遷移方面，外部 Galectin‑3 透過 N‑端域介導的多價結合抑制癌細胞遷移；單獨的糖識別域無此作用。乳糖功能化分枝樹可阻斷 Galectin‑3/糖類相互作用，恢復遷移而不改變細胞存活率，為調節腫瘤擴散提供了潛在治療思路。

## English Integrated Digest

Galectins are versatile carbohydrate‑binding proteins that orchestrate cell–cell and cell–matrix interactions across a spectrum of physiological and pathological settings. Recent studies highlight their emerging influence in immune regulation, metabolic remodeling, cardiac stress responses, and cancer cell behavior.

In adipose tissue, Galectin‑9 is selectively enriched on subcutaneous progenitor cells (APCs) where it promotes an anti‑inflammatory phenotype, whereas CD44 levels fine‑tune APC differentiation into fat cells.  The exosome marker CD63 further enhances extracellular vesicle release and confers resistance to iron‑induced stress, underscoring depot‑specific remodeling mechanisms that may intersect with galectin signaling.  In heart failure, Galectin‑3 (Gal‑3) complements the classic NT‑proBNP biomarker by reflecting biomechanical strain and fibrotic remodeling; together with sST2 it identifies high‑risk phenotypes that natriuretic peptides alone miss, although prospective trials are still needed to prove outcome benefits.

Extracellular Galectin‑3 also exerts a powerful anti‑migration effect on cancer cells through multivalent interactions mediated by its N‑terminal domain.  The carbohydrate recognition domain alone is insufficient, and lactose‑functionalized dendrimers can block this interaction, restoring migration without affecting viability—an insight that could inform novel therapeutic strategies to modulate tumor dissemination.

### English Summary

Patients with autoimmune diseases (AIDs) are at an increased risk of developing cancer; however, the immunometabolic programs that shape this comorbidity remain incompletely defined. We integrated targeted serum metabolomics with multiplex profiling of cytokines, chemokines, and immune checkpoint proteins in patients with five major AIDs-idiopathic inflammatory myopathies, rheumatoid arthritis, systemic lupus erythematosus, Sjögren's syndrome, and systemic sclerosis-with or without cancer. A total of 49 patients with AID and comorbid cancer (AID-CA), 47 patients with AID alone, and 15 healthy controls were enrolled. Compared with AID alone, AID-CA was associated with coordinated remodeling of amino acid and nucleotide metabolism, dominated by the alanine, aspartate, and glutamate pathways. Decreased L-glutamine and increased L-glutamic acid levels were consistently observed and remained significant after the false discovery rate correction. Immune profiling revealed modest but consistent differences, including higher levels of CXCL9 and Galectin-9 and lower levels of selected checkpoint molecules in AID-CA. The effects of major metabolites and immune mediators were directionally stable after adjusting for age, sex, treatment exposure, and autoimmune disease subtype. Integrative analyses revealed stronger associations between metabolites and immune mediators in AID-CA, centered on CXCL9 and Galectin-9, and linked to amino acid and pyrimidine metabolism. In ex vivo assays, perturbation of glutamine or arginine availability preferentially modulated the release of CXCL9 and Galectin-9 from peripheral blood mononuclear cells of patients with AID-CA. Together, these findings define an internally consistent immunometabolic pattern associated with cancer comorbidities in patients with AIDs. They nominated amino acid metabolism and CXCL9/Galectin-9-centered immune signaling as candidates for mechanistic investigation and biomarker development, while underscoring the need for validation in larger, longitudinal, and disease-specific cohorts.

Anaplastic thyroid carcinoma (ATC) is a highly aggressive cancer with poor prognosis, largely due to an immune‑exhausted tumor microenvironment. In this study the authors profiled ATC tissues and identified a striking enrichment of TIM‑3–expressing exhausted cytotoxic T lymphocytes (CTLs), whereas PD‑1 expression was comparatively low. This observation suggested that TIM‑3 may be a more relevant checkpoint in ATC than PD‑1, potentially explaining the limited success of anti‑PD‑1/PD‑L1 therapies.

These findings provide preclinical evidence that TIM‑3 blockade can restore CTL function in ATC and may serve as an alternative or complementary strategy to PD‑1 inhibition. The study supports further investigation of TIM‑3 inhibitors in clinical trials for patients with advanced thyroid cancer.

**Relevance:** TIM‑3 is a lectin-like immune checkpoint that, like galectins, modulates T cell exhaustion and tumor immune evasion. White adipose tissue is divided into distinct anatomical depots that differ in their inflammatory and remodeling characteristics, yet the intrinsic cellular mechanisms underlying these differences are poorly understood. Nonetheless, the data underscore the value of proteomic profiling for uncovering depot‑specific regulatory mechanisms.

Overall, this research advances our understanding of galectin biology within adipose tissue and identifies potential therapeutic targets to modulate inflammation and remodeling in obesity and related metabolic disorders.

**Key findings**

### English Summary

Heart failure (HF) remains a major cause of morbidity and mortality despite advances in neurohormonal therapy. Current clinical assessments, largely based on symptoms and imaging, often miss the underlying structural disease progression that drives adverse outcomes. This narrative review examined literature from 2006 to 2026 to evaluate the biological and prognostic roles of three circulating biomarkers—NT‑proBNP, soluble ST2 (sST2), and Galectin‑3 (Gal‑3)—and to propose a multimarker framework for risk stratification.

The authors searched PubMed, Scopus, and Google Scholar, focusing on studies that assessed the clinical utility, phenotypic specificity, and methodological limitations of these biomarkers. NT‑proBNP remains the gold standard for acute hemodynamic assessment, reflecting ventricular wall stress and fluid status. In contrast, sST2 and Gal‑3 provide complementary information: sST2 signals active biomechanical strain and myocardial inflammation, while Gal‑3 indicates interstitial fibrotic remodeling.

Although observational data suggest that elevated sST2 or Gal‑3 levels identify high‑risk HF phenotypes not captured by natriuretic peptides alone, prospective randomized trials demonstrating that biomarker‑guided management improves clinical outcomes are lacking. The authors therefore propose a four‑step multimarker framework—spanning acute diagnosis to dynamic outpatient monitoring—that integrates NT‑proBNP, sST2, and Gal‑3 to better delineate risk profiles.

The review underscores the potential of a synergistic biomarker strategy but cautions that its clinical implementation awaits prospective validation. Until such data are available, the framework remains hypothesis‑generating rather than practice‑changing.

**Key findings**
